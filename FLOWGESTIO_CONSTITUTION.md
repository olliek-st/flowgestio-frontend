# FLOWGESTIO CONSTITUTION

### Governance Doctrine for the FlowGestio Decision Operating System

---

## PREAMBLE

FlowGestio is a cross-sector Decision Operating System engineered with public-sector–grade governance rigor.

Its purpose is to structure, evaluate, and document strategic project decisions in a manner that is:

* Deterministic
* Traceable
* Financially reproducible
* Governance-aligned
* Audit-defensible

Documents are outputs.

Structured reasoning and disciplined decision architecture are the core product.

This Constitution defines the non-negotiable principles governing all present and future modules of FlowGestio.

---

# ARTICLE I — Identity & Scope

1. FlowGestio is not a template engine.
2. FlowGestio is not a generic AI document generator.
3. FlowGestio is a structured decision system.

The platform produces:

* Business Cases (BC01-level governance artifacts)
* Business Plans derived from structured decision data
* Project governance documents
* Planning artifacts
* Executive presentations derived from canonical case data

All derivative outputs must remain consistent with the underlying structured decision logic.

The Business Case is the canonical source of truth.

---

# ARTICLE II — Core Architectural Separation

FlowGestio enforces strict separation between:

1. Screening (admissibility)
2. Scoring (comparative evaluation)
3. Financial computation
4. Regulatory gating
5. Narrative generation

These layers must never merge implicitly.

---

## Section 2.1 — Screening Criteria Definition

* Screening criteria are defined explicitly.
* Screening criteria represent binary gate logic.
* Screening is not weighted.
* Screening answers: “Is this option admissible?”

Criteria must be visible and structured.

---

## Section 2.2 — Option Justification Layer

Each option must:

* Respond to every screening criterion.
* Provide structured inputs where required.
* Provide textual justification where context is necessary.

This layer collects evidence.
It does not compute verdict.

---

## Section 2.3 — Screening Engine

The screening engine:

* Operates deterministically.
* Produces one of:

  * BASELINE
  * VIABLE
  * DISCOUNTED
* Applies no weighting.
* Applies no implicit bias.
* Executes identical logic under identical inputs.

Screening answers:
“Does this option proceed to comparative analysis?”

---

## Section 3 — Weighted Evaluation (Scoring)

Scoring:

* Applies only to admissible options.
* Uses explicit weights.
* Applies transparent formulas.
* Contains no hidden multipliers.
* Must be reproducible externally.

Screening and scoring must never mix.

---

# ARTICLE III — Financial Engine Doctrine

Financial calculations must be:

* Deterministic
* Reproducible
* Unit-consistent
* Transparent

The system must define and enforce:

* Standard rounding policy: 2 decimal precision using banker’s rounding.
* Defined discount rate input.
* Explicit assumption capture.
* No silent recalculation differences across modules.

NPV, ROI, TCO, Payback, and related outputs must be replicable independently using documented formulas.

Financial integrity supersedes narrative convenience.

---

# ARTICLE IV — Override Governance

Overrides are permitted only with formal justification artifacts.

An override must:

1. Include structured rationale text.
2. Be explicitly logged.
3. Preserve original engine output.
4. Maintain traceability of both computed and overridden states.

No silent override is permitted.

Override authority is conceptual, not role-bound at this stage.

Role-based permission structures may be introduced in future system evolution but are not assumed in current doctrine.

---

# ARTICLE V — Regulatory Gating Doctrine

Regulatory packs must:

* Be separable from PMI-aligned governance logic.
* Be industry-gated.
* Produce distinct WARN and BLOCK signals.
* Avoid contaminating scoring weight.
* Surface validation counts clearly.

Regulatory logic may gate admissibility.
It may not manipulate comparative scoring.

---

# ARTICLE VI — Derivative Document Integrity

Business Plans, executive decks, and marketing artifacts:

* Must derive from canonical Business Case data.
* May adjust tone.
* May simplify presentation.
* May not alter financial conclusions.
* May not distort screening outcomes.
* May not suppress material risk.

Narrative flexibility does not permit structural distortion.

---

# ARTICLE VII — Zero-Risk Architectural Principle

FlowGestio prohibits:

* Speculative heuristics.
* Hidden weight modifiers.
* Silent state mutation.
* Non-deterministic branching.
* Opaque logic.

Identical inputs must produce identical outputs.

If ambiguity exists, the system must request clarification rather than infer.

Integrity overrides convenience.

---

# ARTICLE VIII — Operational Mode Doctrine

FlowGestio may operate in three modes:

1. Advisory Mode — Consultant-operated.
2. Hybrid Mode — Co-build with client.
3. Licensed Mode (future) — Organization-operated.

The operating mode does not alter governance doctrine.

Logic remains invariant across modes.

---

# ARTICLE IX — Evolution & Versioning Doctrine

This Constitution is version-controlled with intent.

Each modification must document:

* What changed.
* Why it changed.
* Trigger source:

  * Regulatory update
  * Client requirement
  * Error correction
  * Product evolution
  * Strategic pivot

Doctrine evolution must be explicit.

Silent conceptual drift is prohibited.

The Constitution supersedes feature convenience.

---

# ARTICLE X — Non-Negotiable Principle

FlowGestio prioritizes:

1. Structural coherence.
2. Financial determinism.
3. Governance traceability.
4. Compliance rigor.
5. Audit defensibility.

Velocity, aesthetic preference, or client pressure must not override these principles.

---

# ARTICLE XI — Glossary of Defined Terms

The following terms have precise meanings within FlowGestio doctrine. They must not be reinterpreted implicitly.

Canonical Source of Truth
The authoritative structured data set from which all derivative artifacts (Business Plans, presentations, summaries) are generated.

Screening
Binary admissibility evaluation determining whether an option proceeds to comparative scoring.

Scoring
Weighted multi-criteria evaluation applied only to admissible options.

BASELINE
The status quo option serving as reference condition. It is not automatically viable; it is the comparative anchor.

VIABLE
An option that satisfies all screening criteria and proceeds to comparative evaluation.

DISCOUNTED
An option that fails one or more screening criteria and does not proceed to scoring unless formally overridden.

Override
A formally documented deviation from computed engine output, requiring structured justification and traceable logging.

Regulatory Pack
A structured set of industry-gated validation rules producing WARN and/or BLOCK signals separate from scoring logic.

WARN
A non-blocking signal indicating risk, inconsistency, or missing documentation requiring acknowledgment.

BLOCK
A blocking validation signal preventing progression until resolved.

Deterministic Engine
A computational component that produces identical outputs under identical inputs without hidden modifiers.

Zero-Risk Architectural Principle
The doctrine prohibiting speculative heuristics, opaque weighting, silent mutation, or non-deterministic branching within the system.
